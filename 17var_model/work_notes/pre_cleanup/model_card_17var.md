# Model Card - KCHS→NHANES Depression Screening Model (17-predictor)

## Model details
- **Model**: Gradient-boosting classifier (LightGBM) predicting current depression (PHQ-9 ≥10).
- **Version**: 17-predictor harmonized model, 2026. Fitted on the corrected harmonization and the cluster-level internal split described in the manuscript (Study Design and Data Sources, External Validation and Sensitivity Analyses); all earlier development runs are superseded (`results/_SUPERSEDED.md`).
- **Inputs (17)**: age, sex, education, household income (within-survey quintile), self-rated health, partnership, living alone, smoking, current drinking, physical-activity recommendation met, sleep hours, BMI, hypertension, diabetes, employment, self-rated oral health, food insecurity.
- **Output**: probability of PHQ-9 ≥10 (screen-positive risk).
- **Selection/tuning**: hyperparameters, feature representation, and algorithm all chosen by KCHS-internal cross-validation only (no external data used in fitting, tuning or algorithm selection; the three-predictor amendment is the documented exception and was written after the earlier external result was known). Selected config: num_leaves 31, min_child_samples 1000, learning_rate 0.01, n_estimators 900, reg_alpha 1.0, reg_lambda 20, colsample_bytree 0.6, subsample 0.6 (inactive: subsample_freq left at its default of 0; hence no row subsampling occurs). Chosen from a 24-configuration randomized search × 2 feature representations by 4-fold survey-weighted CV (300,000-record subsample); algorithms compared at each one's tuned best under an identical 5-fold protocol (200,000-record subsample): LightGBM 0.840 > random forest 0.835 > elastic net 0.822.

## Intended use
- **Purpose**: population-level screening/triage aid to prioritize higher-risk individuals for further assessment, deliverable via a low-burden web/mobile self-report form.
- **Intended users**: public-health and primary-care screening programs; researchers.
- **Out of scope**: NOT a diagnostic device; not for standalone clinical decisions; not validated for individual-level clinical determination, for populations outside community-dwelling adults ≥20 years, or without local recalibration.

## Factors
- Evaluated across sex, age band, education, household income quintile (KCHS) and race/ethnicity (NHANES). Performance is lower in younger adults (20-39 y) and in some US minority subgroups (see Interpretability, Fairness, and Clinical Utility).

## Training data
- KCHS 2017-2025, community-dwelling adults ≥20 y with complete PHQ-9; pooled n=2,048,510; weighted depression prevalence 3.6%. Complex-sample (stratified, clustered, weighted). Clusters (year x design stratum x primary sampling unit) were assigned to the hold-out first, giving 100,001 records; the model was then fitted on 500,000 records drawn at random from the remaining clusters, and internal metrics were computed on the hold-out.

## Evaluation data
- NHANES pre-pandemic (2017-March 2020; n=7,888; prevalence 8.4%) and post-pandemic (2021-2023; n=5,210; prevalence 12.2%). Applied without refitting.

## Ethical considerations
- Public, de-identified secondary data; no re-identification. Low PPV means many screen-positives are not depressed, and the score must be paired with confirmatory assessment and support pathways, not automated action. Subgroup performance differences (age, race/ethnicity) require monitoring to avoid inequitable triage.

## Caveats and recommendations
- **Deploy only with local recalibration**: discrimination transports, but calibration drifts across country and pandemic; intercept/slope recalibration on local data restores it.
- **Cross-national ceiling ~0.77**: external discrimination did not exceed ~0.77 in any leakage-controlled attempt, and an in-domain oracle fitted directly to NHANES reached only 0.764-0.776 against 0.81-0.82 in equally sized Korean samples. Concept shift is the most consistent explanation, but differing measurement, development-survey coverage and complete-case selection cannot be separated from concept shift in these data.
- **Food insecurity** is the most weakly harmonized predictor (single item vs 10-item module; 4-5× prevalence difference); results are robust to its removal (16-predictor model).
- Self-reported inputs; cross-sectional (screens current symptoms, not future onset).
- Software: Python 3.14 with scikit-learn 1.9.0, lightgbm 4.6.0, shap 0.52.0, numpy 2.4.5, pandas 3.0.3, scipy 1.18.0, pyreadstat 1.3.5 and matplotlib 3.10.9. Complex-survey weighting, the stratified cluster bootstrap, calibration measures and decision-curve net benefit are implemented directly rather than through a survey-analysis package.
